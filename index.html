Say for example, Saturation.

It's a simple concept, but it applies to a lot of disciplines in several branches of science, qualitative research, medicine, colors, art, music, business and a lot more. In fact, saturation exists in your mind as applied in as something you must ventilate to make space for information to circulate again.

  • In Physics & Chemistry: A liquid solvent hits its absolute limit of solute; no more sugar dissolves, it just pools at the dead bottom.
  • In Color Theory: A hue reaches its maximum purity and intensity before it washes out into blinding white or heavy gray.
  • In Audio Engineering: Pushing a signal until the analog waveform clips into distortion—holding maximum amplitude right before the system breaks.
  • In Market Economics: Market saturation occurs when every potential buyer holds the product, and pumping in more supply yields zero growth.
  • In Cognitive Architecture: Working memory (RAM) maxes out under simultaneous multi-threaded pipelines until processing halts—mirroring the exact mental bottleneck we bypass using .
